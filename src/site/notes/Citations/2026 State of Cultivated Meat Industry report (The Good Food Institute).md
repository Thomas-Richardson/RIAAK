# Executive Summary / Abstract
- Regulatory path-to-market milestones brought new products to consumers in 2025, including cultivated foie gras, pork fat, and salmon.
- The private funding environment tightened significantly, with cultivated meat and seafood companies raising $73.9 million in 2025, down from $139 million in 2024.
- Commercial production is beginning to scale, highlighted by the opening of a 35,000-liter capacity facility in Sydney, Australia.
- Governments facing supply chain constraints are increasingly integrating cultivated meat into national food strategies and biotechnology plans.
- Formal regulatory pathways for cultivated foods now exist in Australia-New Zealand, Singapore, and the United States.
- Investors are showing increased discretion, prioritizing companies that demonstrate progress on fundamentals like cost, taste, and scale.
- Industry metrics such as bioreactor capacity in operation, sustained output over time, and run reliability were strengthened throughout the year.
- Some startups, such as IntegriCulture, achieved profitability by diversifying revenue through the sale of research tools and nonfood products.
- The first publicly available set of suspension-adapted bovine cell lines was released to academia and industry to save R&D time and expenses.
- Vow achieved production at a 20,000-liter scale, reaching a production level comparable to the largest biopharma processes.
- Challenges remain regarding production efficiency, consumer awareness, and the lack of reliable data points for the cost of equipment and infrastructure.
- China's State Development & Investment Corporation committed over CNY 4 billion ($555 million) to advance biomanufacturing and "new proteins."
- Federal R&D investment for cultivated meat in the United States declined as the government scaled back funding for biotechnology and biomanufacturing.
- A total of seven companies globally have now received regulatory clearance to sell various cultivated meat products.
- The underlying case for the sector remains strong, driven by rising meat demand, climate and land use pressures, and the need to diversify protein supply chains.
