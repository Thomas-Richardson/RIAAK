# Executive Summary / Abstract
[From Executive Summary]

- Cumulative government investment in alternative proteins has grown from approximately $700 million in 2021 to at least $2.5 billion as of 2025.
- The number of countries working to advance alternative proteins has more than doubled, increasing from 16 to at least 33 in the last five years.
- Governments are increasingly incorporating alternative proteins into national economic, biotechnology, and food security strategies.
- Trade barriers and global uncertainty have led policymakers to invest in resilient domestic food supply chains through plant-based proteins, fermentation, and cultivated meat.
- New research programs in countries like India, New Zealand, and Spain are investigating uses for local agricultural sidestreams to create new markets for farmers.
- China and the European Union led the world in new investments in 2025, prioritizing biomanufacturing as a strategic priority.
- China’s state-owned State Development and Investment Corporation announced nearly $1 billion in biotechnology facility investments, including food applications.
- The European Union awarded over €100 million ($113 million) in loans to plant-based and fermentation companies for scaling up.
- The United States federal government significantly drew back from food biotechnology investments, announcing only $6 million in new research funding.
- Australia, Canada, China, India, and South Korea are incentivizing the construction of shared biomanufacturing facilities to help startups move from research to production.
- Food Standards Australia New Zealand (FSANZ) approved a cultivated quail product, making it the third jurisdiction globally to greenlight cultivated meat.
- The United States gave regulatory clearance to the first cultivated seafood and cultivated pork products in 2025.
- South Korea, Abu Dhabi, China, Japan, and the United Kingdom are in various stages of developing dedicated regulatory processes for cultivated food products.
- Labeling debates persist regarding the use of "meaty" terms for plant-based products, though research suggests consumers find these terms informative rather than misleading.
- Regional investment trends show the Asia Pacific focusing on cultivated meat, while Europe and the Americas invest more heavily in plant-based proteins.
- Europe leads the world in the development and production of plant-based and fermentation-enabled cheese.
- Current global government investment amounts to no more than 10 percent of the $10.1 billion annual benchmark needed to fully reap the sector's economic and environmental rewards.
