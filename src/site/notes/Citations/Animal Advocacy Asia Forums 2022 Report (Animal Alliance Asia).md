# Executive Summary / Abstract
[From Summary section]

- Local animal advocates (LAAs) across ten countries identified significant gaps in financial resources, advocacy training, and country-specific knowledge.
- The lack of funding prevents the creation of paid positions, making animal advocacy a difficult career path to pursue sustainably.
- Advocates expressed a strong need for training in leadership, nonprofit management, fundraising, and communication skills.
- Mental health support is considered vital to address burnout and ensure the long-term sustainability of the movement.
- There is a desire for middle entities to facilitate collaboration, resource sharing, and volunteer networks between organizations.
- A significant data gap exists regarding local animal agriculture systems, welfare violations, and specific advocacy tactics.
- Social media engagement and on-site activities are the most used tactics because they are affordable and accessible.
- Very few advocates currently engage with government agencies or public officials to influence laws and official policies.
- Friendly and supportive methods, such as amicable corporate engagement and sympathy-based campaigns, are perceived as the most effective.
- Some advocates prioritize gaining public support for widely accepted causes, such as wildlife or companion animal protection, before tackling controversial topics like farmed animal rights.
- Capacity-building organizations are needed to provide incubation programs for new organizations and support for grassroots initiatives.
- Reliable and consistent grants are necessary to cover project expenses and attract skilled individuals to the movement.
- While international funders are generally viewed positively, there are concerns regarding the transparency of fund distribution through middle entities.
- Some advocates worry that international funding might dictate local strategies, limiting the ability to adapt to specific cultural contexts.
- Funding opportunities are valued for helping local advocates connect with international animal advocacy organizations and improve strategic planning.
