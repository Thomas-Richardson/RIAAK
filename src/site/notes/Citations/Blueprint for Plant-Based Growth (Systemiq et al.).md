# Executive Summary / Abstract
[From Executive Summary]

- The UK plant-based market is showing positive signs of resurgence and sustained long-term growth when viewed through a nuanced commercial lens.
- The commercial opportunity has diversified; meat alternatives, dairy alternatives, and ingredient-led proteins now play different roles and require distinct activation strategies.
- Dairy alternatives remain established and perform well, while ingredient-led proteins (tofu, tempeh, beans, and pulses) are gaining traction with shoppers.
- While plant-based products are widely stocked, signposting, meal-occasion framing, and category adjacencies remain inconsistent across UK retailers.
- The next phase of growth will be shaped by how effectively the category is connected to shopper missions and store navigation rather than availability alone.
- Distribution has scaled faster than category activation, leaving an execution gap in shopper communication and meal-occasion framing.
- Ingredient-led proteins represent an under-activated growth space that aligns with shopper interest in protein, fibre, value, and scratch cooking.
- Price parity is a direct commercial lever, but trust, quality, and usage confidence are just as important for driving repeat purchases.
- Retailers should move from "stocked" to "scaled" by improving fixture cues, navigation, and connecting products to routine everyday meals.
- Integrating plant-based ingredients into all project briefs and protein portfolio considerations can drive volume and build supply resilience.
- Smart adjacencies and integrated placement (similar to dairy alternatives) can reduce shopper friction and increase basket size.
- Loyalty schemes and digital platforms should be utilized to nudge non-category buyers toward plant-based options through meal-occasion prompts.
- Key calendar moments and new meal occasions can be used to normalize plant-based products by focusing on taste and excitement.
- Integrating plant-based buying teams can help optimize range, space, and margin across the full protein fixture.
- Shared, non-sensitive sector knowledge and measurement can strengthen category evidence and sustain long-term growth.
