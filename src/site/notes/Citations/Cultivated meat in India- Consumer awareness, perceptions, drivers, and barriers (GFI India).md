### Overview
As cultivated meat emerges as a novel food technology globally, understanding consumer readiness is imperative to informing product development, industry strategy, and regulatory decision-making. This study explores perceptions of cultivated meat among younger, educated Indian non-vegetarian consumers from four metro cities—a segment identified as being among the category’s most likely early adopters. It explores their existing awareness, responses after the concept is explained, interest in trying cultivated meat, and the practical and trust-related considerations that could influence adoption.

### Quick insights
*   **Attitudes and concerns**: Top drivers for trial are environmental benefits, reduced contamination risk, and animal welfare. Top barriers are lack of familiarity, fear of long-term health risks, and concerns about 'naturalness'.
*   **Awareness and familiarity**: 95% had not heard of cultivated meat before the survey, but 59% would be willing to try it after learning about the concept.
*   **Intent to try and purchase**: 63% would be likely or very likely to buy cultivated meat if it were available.
*   **Category nomenclature**: Terms like 'clean meat' and 'cell-based meat' resonate most, while 'lab-grown meat' has negative connotations.
*   **Suitability**: 73% perceive cultivated meat as a good alternative to conventional meat.
*   **Believability**: 69% find the concept of cultivated meat believable.
*   **Uniqueness**: 83% consider cultivated meat to be different from plant-based meat.
