## Overview
The report has no formal executive summary; this is the report's own description of itself (section 1.1 and the CSCP publication page).

The LIKE-A-PRO behavioural intervention pilots explored how different behavioural strategies could promote the uptake of alternative proteins (APs). Each pilot focused on one of four behavioural pathways: choice editing (reducing the visibility or availability of conventional protein options while maintaining consumer autonomy), choice expansion (introducing APs in more prominent and appealing ways), choice environment (changes in presentation, naming or positioning) and beyond choice (influencing perceptions and intentions before the moment of choice).

The pilots were implemented in Greece (institutional catering, choice editing), Norway (retail, choice expansion), Spain and Belgium (restaurants, choice environment), and the Netherlands, Denmark and Spain (digital communication, beyond choice).

## Key findings (section 4 of the report)
- Behaviour changes most reliably when the food environment reduces friction, preserves autonomy, and presents AP options as attractive, normal, and easy to integrate into routine decisions.
- Trial is not the same as sustained change. Structural and communicative interventions can activate short-term uptake and first-time engagement, but longer-term integration depends on repeat purchase, trust in product quality, economic feasibility for providers, and alignment with habitual routines.
- The most robust levers were opportunity (availability, salience, parity placement, integrated menus) and motivation (sensory appeal, gain framing, non-moralising positioning). Capability-building worked best when embedded in the environment (tastings, staff interaction).

## Pilot results (figures from the report)

### Pilot 1 - Choice editing, university canteen, Greece
- Stepwise reduction of meat dishes over two intervention weeks (20-24 Oct 2025, 5 days; 18-21 Nov 2025, 4 days), baseline 15-17 Oct 2025.
- Repeated cross-sectional surveys: N = 684 (baseline n = 171; week 1 n = 307; week 2 n = 206), plus 4 interviews with food actors. Sample mean age 23.9, mostly university students, 57% male, about 80% omnivorous.
- About 71% of respondents ate at the canteen during intervention weeks, and 51.4% of those chose a plant-based meal.
- In week 2, 30.7% said they ate elsewhere because of the intervention and 42.9% felt moderately or very restricted.
- Awareness of APs rose from 55.6% to about 81%; attitudes stayed moderate and stable (about 3.3-3.4 out of 5), and intentions did not change significantly (about 2.5 out of 5). No significant reduction in meat consumption frequency.
- Respondents said better taste/quality (66.3%) and lower prices (60.5%) were the most effective levers.

### Pilot 2 - Choice expansion, supermarkets, Norway (Coop, Trondheim region)
- Two featured AP products (Veggie Falafel, Veggie Burger) moved to high-salience campaign placement with discounts, plus tastings in one store; control week (wk 44) vs intervention week (wk 45) 2025, three stores.
- Burger sales rose from 3 to 81 units; falafel from 10 to 171 units (265 units across both weeks). Conventional meat products promoted in the same coolers also rose strongly and stayed far higher in absolute volume.
- Survey N = 204 (mostly social media recruitment); 808 tasting samples distributed; 6 retailer interviews. Retailers described processed AP products as a small and declining category in Norway.

### Pilot 3 - Choice environment, restaurants, Spain (LABe) and Belgium (Bar Amelia)
- Spain: 6 weekends (Nov-Dec 2025); Belgium: 2 weeks (Nov 2025). Integrated vs segregated menu placement of one AP dish.
- AP dish share of sales: LABe integrated 20 of 257 (8%) vs segregated 12 of 204 (6%); Bar Amelia integrated 38 of 376 (10%) vs segregated 16 of 209 (8%).
- Surveys N = 319. About half noticed highlighted dishes (Spain 46%, Belgium 50%); about 75% preferred sensory/emotional descriptions. The report calls the sales effects modest.

### Pilot 4 - Beyond choice, Meta ads, Netherlands, Denmark and Spain
- Three-week campaign testing gain, loss and priming framings (health and sustainability angles). Reach 37,781 (NL 12,313; DK 12,458; ES 13,010), 1,663 link clicks, 487 recipe downloads, average click-through rate 3.27%, average cost per download EUR 2.75.
- Gain framing performed best and loss framing worst. Downloads are a proxy for interest, not confirmed cooking or eating. Also 20 interviews with campaign professionals across 11 European countries.

## Limitations stated by the report
- Short interventions (days to weeks), no long-term follow-up; results described as indicative rather than definitive.
- Small, single-site or two-site pilots; canteen sample was mostly young students; self-reported measures; bundled intervention components (cannot separate effects of menu changes from messaging).
- Retail and restaurant sales are raw counts without statistical testing; conventional meat also rose in the retail pilot.
- Meta delivery was algorithm-optimised, not randomised (quasi-experimental).
- The report was written by the consortium that ran the pilots (CSCP plus partner research bodies); reviewers were also consortium partners, so it is not independent peer review. Funded by the EU Horizon Europe programme, Grant Agreement No. 101083961.

## Notes on how Vegconomist reported it
- Vegconomist gives no sample sizes or numbers; all figures above come from the report itself.
- Vegconomist says the pilots ran 'across 11 European countries'. The report's pilots ran in Greece, Norway, Spain, Belgium, the Netherlands and Denmark (six countries). Eleven is the number of countries of the campaign professionals interviewed in the beyond-choice pilot.
- Vegconomist's line that taste, price and trust decide whether consumers return is the report's cross-pilot conclusion, but the pilots themselves measured short-term trial, not return purchases.
