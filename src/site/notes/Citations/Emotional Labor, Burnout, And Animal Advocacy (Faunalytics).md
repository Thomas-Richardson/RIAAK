This article explores the issues of emotional labor, burnout, and compassion fatigue within animal advocacy. While emotions are a key tool for persuasion, the emotional well-being of advocates is often downplayed. 

\\"Burnout\\" is a chronic emotional distress that hinders effectiveness, driven by factors like the burden of awareness, infighting, and a \\"culture of martyrdom.\\" The intense emotional labor of advocacy can lead to negative coping mechanisms like emotional detachment and compassion fatigue.

The article argues for a shift from individual self-care to community care, stressing that organizations must create supportive ecosystems to combat burnout and ensure the movement's sustainability. It highlights that advocates may leave the movement due to the very positive traits (empathy, commitment) that initially drew them in, if those traits are not supported.
