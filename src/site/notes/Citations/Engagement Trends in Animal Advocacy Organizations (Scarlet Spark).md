# Executive Summary / Abstract
[From Summary section]

- Nine in ten people feel safe from harassment, understand their role, know why their work matters, and get along well with their coworkers.
- Workplace experience is closely tied to intent to stay; the bottom-scoring group of participants had a 62% risk of leaving, while only 3% in the top-scoring group expressed intent to leave.
- Leadership and learning are the biggest opportunities to increase engagement and retention.
- Confidence in leaders (50.5% strongly agree) and open communication (49.3% strongly agree) are key drivers for organizational recommendation.
- Concerns with senior leadership are the most frequent issues named by those scoring low on intent to stay.
- A belief in the mission is not an antidote to problems with leadership; mission-aligned staff are still at risk of leaving if they experience friction with leaders.
- Belonging is the strongest predictor of engagement and retention, but it is not felt equally across all groups.
- Neurodivergent respondents score significantly lower on belonging (50% strongly agree) than others (68% strongly agree).
- Disabled respondents report lower belonging (38%) and less say in decisions (21%) compared to the overall population.
- Men report experiencing more say in decision-making than women (54% against 43% strongly agree), though women represent 67% of participants.
- People three to five years into a job are the least likely to feel heard (34% strongly agree).
- Relationships alone do not produce belonging; it also requires feeling heard, treated fairly, and appreciated.
- Workplace experience accounts for 69% of a respondent's willingness to recommend their organization.
- Most people remain committed to the animal advocacy movement even if they plan to leave their specific organization; only 3.2% plan to leave the movement entirely.
- Having positive relationships across the animal protection movement is a stronger predictor of movement commitment than any workplace experience factor.
- As pay rises, intent to stay in the movement slightly declines for all but the top-paid roles, likely due to the scarcity of high-paid advocacy positions.
