# Executive Summary / Abstract
[AI-derived summary]

*   **Entrenching Factory Farming:** The genetic engineering (GE) of agricultural animals normalizes the harmful conditions of industrial factory farming—such as overcrowding, pollution, and disease—by redesigning animals to endure suffering rather than reforming the underlying agricultural system.
*   **Recent Approvals:** Between 2020 and 2025, the U.S. FDA approved three GE animals for human consumption: Acceligen’s heat-resistant 'PRLR-SLICK' cattle, Revivicor’s allergy-modified 'GalSafe' pig, and Genus PLC’s disease-resistant 'PRRS' pig.
*   **Severe Welfare Harms in Development:** The techniques used to create GE animals (cloning and micro-injection) are highly inefficient and cause significant animal suffering, physical abnormalities, and high mortality rates. 
*   **High Failure Rates and Mortality:** Developing the GalSafe pigs required thousands of cloned embryos to achieve just two successful pregnancies, and the resulting pigs suffered double the pre-weaning mortality rate of conventional pigs (25% vs. 12.6%). 
*   **Discarded Animals:** Only 20% of the PRRS-resistant piglets carried the intended genetic edit, leading to numerous animals being discarded due to abnormalities. Similarly, PRLR-SLICK cattle trials resulted in extensive failures, including a calf dying from an unexpected heart defect.
*   **Unintended Genetic Mutations:** GE processes routinely cause off-target genetic mutations that disrupt normal biological functions, leading to chronic pain, immune compromise, and early death. For example, the mutation in PRLR-SLICK cattle disrupts a hormone receptor involved in over 300 biological processes, with unknown long-term welfare impacts.
*   **Zoonotic Disease Risks:** Engineering virus-resistant animals (like the PRRS pig) places evolutionary pressure on pathogens. This risks driving the mutation of more virulent, transmissible viruses capable of bypassing the resistance and potentially spilling over into human populations.
*   **Antibiotic Resistance:** The GalSafe pig contains an antibiotic-resistant marker gene. This resistance could transfer to gut bacteria and spread into the environment or food chain, potentially making human infections harder to treat. 
*   **Food Safety Gaps:** The FDA failed to evaluate the safety of milk from PRLR-SLICK cattle because the genetic edit mimicked a naturally occurring protein, ignoring the potential for altered proteins to cause allergic reactions in humans.
*   **Inadequate Regulatory Oversight:** U.S. regulation of GE animals is highly fragmented across the FDA, USDA, and EPA, with no single agency assessing cumulative impacts on animal welfare or ecosystems. The FDA relies almost entirely on company-generated data, lacks independent scientific review, and does not require assessments of harm to experimental "founder" animals.
*   **Weak Labeling Rules:** USDA rules limit consumer transparency by only requiring disclosure when GE DNA is detectable (leaving many gene-edited products unlabeled) and allowing disclosures to be hidden behind QR codes rather than printed clearly on packaging. 
*   **Systemic Alternatives:** The report concludes that GE animals are a "false solution" and a dangerous distraction. It urges policymakers to halt GE animal approvals and instead invest in agroecology, organic, and well-managed pasture-based systems that structurally improve animal welfare and eliminate the need for routine antibiotics.
