# Executive Summary / Abstract
[From Summary section]

- Concern about farm animal welfare is widely shared, with 87% of UK adults saying it is important that the welfare of farmed animals is protected in the UK.
- One in six UK adults place farm animal welfare among the Government's five highest political priorities.
- A political commitment to advancing farm animal welfare is more likely to create political upside than downside: 43% of voters would be more likely to support such a party, while only 5% would be less likely.
- Public support is strongest for clear, practical reforms such as stronger welfare laws, welfare labelling, and non-stun slaughter labelling.
- Import restrictions receive net-positive support, though they attract lower support than domestic welfare measures and labelling.
- Factual information can sharpen public opinion; after reading a description of farrowing crates, 51% of respondents favored prohibiting their use compared to 25% who supported permitting them.
- 57% of respondents support a complete ban on cages for farmed animals.
- The public expects shared responsibility for funding higher welfare standards, with retailers and supermarkets (36%) and the Government (22%) most frequently selected as the main sources of support.
- A majority of consumers say they would pay more for higher welfare products: 59% for meat, 63% for eggs and 65% for dairy. The most common level is up to 5% more (27% for meat, 28% for eggs, 28% for dairy).
- 42% of respondents believe industrial farming is more harmful to animals than traditional farming, while 47% believe industrial producers negatively impact small-scale farms.
- Support for improving farm animal welfare is a mainstream public value that extends across all major political parties.
