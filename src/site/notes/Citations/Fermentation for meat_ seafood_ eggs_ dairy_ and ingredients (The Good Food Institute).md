# Executive Summary / Abstract
[From Executive Summary]

- In 2025, the fermentation-enabled protein and ingredients sector saw new product launches across meat, dairy, and ingredients, along with regulatory milestones in China and the United States.
- The funding environment tightened significantly, with companies in the fermentation ecosystem raising $357 million in 2025, down from $651 million in 2024.
- Production quantities are increasing as new commercial-scale locations opened in Brazil, Canada, China, Sweden, and the United Kingdom.
- Public investment is expanding as governments recognize fermentation's potential to address supply chain constraints, food system resilience, and national security.
- Partnerships between startups and established corporations in the dairy, meat, and yeast industries are helping to maximize viability and efficiency in product pipelines.
- While biomass fermentation products are becoming more widely available, product availability for precision fermentation remains more limited.
- Europe is positioning itself as a strategic hub for the fermentation bioeconomy by leveraging public investment in infrastructure and permitting pathways.
- Recent high-profile closures and restructurings have sharpened investor focus on unit economics, sustainable demand, and credible paths to profitability.
- Research collaborations have identified critical steps toward improving strain stability, productivity, and quality for mycoprotein species at scale.
- Continuous fermentation systems are maturing, with some pilots achieving threefold productivity gains and a 50 percent reduction in costs.
- Data standardization is increasingly needed to enable AI and machine learning process optimization across the fermentation sector.
- Publicly funded infrastructure is playing a key role in de-risking process development and scale-up for new fermentation-derived products.
- Intellectual property disputes regarding foundational technologies signal a commercialization risk that could slow sector-wide scaling.
- South Korea enacted the Food Tech Industry Promotion Act to establish a legal framework for supporting innovative food products and businesses.
- EU policymakers agreed to ban the use of "meat" and 31 meat-related terms for alternative proteins, creating potential labeling challenges for the industry.
