# Executive Summary / Abstract
[From Executive Summary]

- Public momentum to phase out cages in EU farming has intensified following the 2021 European Citizens’ Initiative “End the Cage Age” and the Commission’s expected revision of animal-welfare legislation in late 2026.
- The transition to cage-free systems is technically feasible but involves significant structural investments, management changes, and land or building adaptations.
- Structural investments dominate transition costs, while productivity typically rebounds after an initial learning period, reinforcing the importance of phased timelines.
- The maturity of alternative systems varies by species; laying hen alternatives are technically mature, while group housing for breeding rabbits requires further optimization.
- Existing EU and national policy instruments are currently insufficiently targeted to facilitate cage‑free transitions at a large scale.
- Legislative certainty with clear transition deadlines is identified as the primary driver of investment readiness for farmers.
- Uncertainty regarding forthcoming EU legislation and timelines currently makes many farmers hesitant to invest in housing systems that could become obsolete.
- Upfront capital for construction, retrofits, and land reconfiguration remains the binding constraint across all species and Member States.
- In the pig sector, well-designed free-farrowing systems can achieve performance comparable to cages after a learning curve, with production cost increases estimated at roughly 2–4%.
- For calves, the operational costs of collective housing tend to be similar to those of individual pens, though upfront investment for facility remodelling may be required.
- Current Common Agricultural Policy (CAP) support largely rewards the maintenance of existing practices rather than the transformation required for cage-free systems.
- The proposed 2028–2034 CAP introduces transition payments, but the removal of ring-fenced budgets for animal welfare poses a risk to the attractiveness of these schemes.
- Demand-side measures, such as harmonized EU‑wide animal‑welfare labelling and public procurement, are currently underused but essential for sharing costs along the value chain.
- Retail commitments and industry-led bans, as seen in the German laying hen sector, have proven instrumental in facilitating legal bans and market transitions.
- The report recommends the swift adoption of revised EU animal welfare legislation and the establishment of species-specific "conversion packages" that include technical standards and staff training.
