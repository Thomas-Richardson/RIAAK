# Executive Summary / Abstract
[From Executive Summary]

- The Common Agricultural Policy (CAP) represents approximately EUR 386.6 billion for the 2021-2027 period, playing a central role in shaping EU farm structures and land markets.
- Direct payments, specifically the Basic Income Support for Sustainability (BISS), constitute the structural backbone of support, accounting for roughly 51% of the direct payments’ envelope.
- By linking support primarily to land area, the current model may contribute to land concentration and rising prices, potentially hindering access for young farmers and new entrants.
- Research suggests approximately 82% of EU agricultural subsidies are linked to animal products, either directly through livestock (38%) or indirectly through feed crops (44%).
- Two-thirds of EU cereal production is used for animal feed, contributing to a structural dependence where the EU imports around 70% of its high-protein feed needs.
- Support for plant-based protein systems for human consumption remains limited, currently representing only 4-5% of coupled income support.
- Agriculture accounts for 11% of total EU greenhouse gas emissions, with these emissions heavily concentrated in livestock production systems.
- The CAP’s current allocation model is increasingly misaligned with EU legal obligations regarding environmental integration, climate neutrality, and animal welfare.
- Recent developments in climate and environmental litigation signal a growing shift toward enforceable accountability for public agricultural spending.
- The report proposes reallocating 20% of the support that currently benefits livestock and feed-dependent systems toward plant-based proteins for direct human consumption.
- This proposed reallocation is presented as a fiscally neutral, structurally transformative lever to improve policy coherence and strategic autonomy in the post-2027 reform.
- Successful food transition requires that CAP reform be accompanied by demand-side measures such as public procurement, market incentives, and updated dietary guidelines.
