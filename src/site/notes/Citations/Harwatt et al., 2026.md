## Abstract

Governments, retailers and investors are increasingly deploying protein strategies to align food systems with climate, biodiversity and public-health goals, yet these strategies lack a common quantitative benchmark. Using the FABIO multi-regional physical input–output database, we translate the EAT-Lancet Planetary Health Diet (PHD) into country-level protein benchmarks for consumption, production and trade, and assess the implied reallocation of protein across countries and food groups. Global per capita protein consumption is largely unchanged (78 g/day), but a substantial protein gap exists. Plant-sourced protein must increase by 28% and animal-sourced protein must fall by 35%. Five “Super Producer” countries and five “Super Consumer” countries each account for half the global total, making them potential early leverage points for protein policy. PHD-aligned production would reduce food-system GHG emissions by 16% and land use by 33% (1,170 Mha). Results are directly relevant to existing and emerging national protein strategies and retailer protein sales targets.
