# Executive Summary / Abstract
[From Executive Summary]

- People are caught between rising hope in what science can do and rising anxiety about the rapid pace of global change.
- Breakthrough technologies feel powerful and full of promise, yet also distant, opaque, and difficult for the public to judge.
- People do not react to technology itself, but to what it represents regarding control, naturalness, fairness, and human identity.
- Three distinct mindsets shape reactions: Optimists (see science as an engine of progress), Rationalists (balance risks and accountability), and Skeptics (protect values like autonomy and authenticity).
- These mindsets are fluid; individuals may be optimistic about one technology (like AI) while remaining skeptical of another (like cultivated meat).
- Most people want scientific progress to feel human, fair, and safe rather than purely technical.
- Public acceptance of AI depends on it supporting human professionals, such as doctors, rather than replacing them.
- Cell and Gene Therapies (CGT) are welcomed for their potential to cure suffering, provided they do not create new societal divides.
- New Genomic Techniques (NGTs) in agriculture are supported when seen as protecting the planet rather than just benefiting corporations.
- Cultivated meat faces a high bar for transparency to overcome being perceived as mysterious or misleading.
- Gen Z is the most optimistic generation but experiences intense tension regarding the loss of personal agency in a world of algorithms.
- Trust varies by region: it is high and institutionally anchored in China, fragmented and polarized in the US, and stable but conditional in Germany.
- Unfamiliarity with NGTs and cultivated meat often leads to neutrality, suggesting people are open to being guided by honest communication.
- Technologies that people can experience directly, like AI, generate much stronger opinions than intangible ones like NGTs.
- There is a significant desire for trusted expert guidance from scientists and doctors to help interpret complex, often contradictory, information.
- Acceptance of innovation accelerates when technologies are seen to provide collective benefits, such as solving environmental challenges.
- Healthcare innovations are typically evaluated through personal impact, whereas food and climate technologies are viewed through a lens of shared future well-being.
- Building societal alignment requires innovators to communicate with empathy and demonstrate how technology strengthens human agency.
