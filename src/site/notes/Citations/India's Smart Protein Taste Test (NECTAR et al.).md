# India's Smart Protein Taste Test: A Blind Sensory Analysis of Meat and Egg Alternatives (NECTAR, GFI India, India Animal Fund; October 2026)

Published by NECTAR (a research initiative of Food System Innovations), the Good Food Institute India and India Animal Fund. Released 5 November 2026. Licensed CC BY-NC 4.0. Landing page and data dashboard: https://www.nectar.org/sensory-research/2026-taste-buds

Related: [[Citations/Taste of the Industry 2026 (NECTAR et al.)\|Taste of the Industry 2026 (NECTAR et al.)]] and [[Citations/Taste of the Industry 2025 (NECTAR)\|Taste of the Industry 2025 (NECTAR)]] (the same NECTAR blind-taste method applied to other products), [[Alt proteins in Asia\|Alt proteins in Asia]].

# Executive Summary / Abstract

A blind taste test of plant-based meat and eggs with Indian consumers. Plant-based products were generally well liked and appealed in concept about as much as animal products, but they trail animal benchmarks on taste, mostly because of flavor. Two categories reached taste parity at the level of the best product. Eggs, chicken chunks and mutton keema have the furthest to go.

## Method
- 700 omnivores (average age 30, aged 18–45, 50:50 men and women, 50:50 Mumbai and Bangalore, mostly NCCS A1/A2, i.e. higher-income urban households), recruited as young urban people who regularly eat meat or eggs.
- 26 plant-based products across 8 categories, each against one animal benchmark of the same type, format and storage condition. Categories picked from a landscape analysis of the Indian market, prioritised by maturity.
- Seven panels of 100 people. Each panel rated 4–6 products from one category, blind and in rotated order. (Chicken tikka and mutton seekh kebabs shared one panel.) Eaten in a familiar dish, such as butter chicken gravy with chapati, pan-cooked keema, or egg bhurji with bread.
- Ipsos ran the central-location test. Palate supported the study design and analysis.
- Ratings on a 1–7 liking scale. Parity was judged with a Wilcoxon signed-rank test plus a Bayesian estimate.

## Taste performance
- **Overall:** 60% rated plant-based products "like" or "like very much" (only 11% disliked them), against 78% for animal benchmarks. Mean liking 5.5 vs 6.1. Animal benchmarks were 1.7 times more likely to get "like very much" (39% vs 23%).
- **Taste parity:** the leading plant-based chicken patty (6.0 vs 6.1; 70% rated it the same or better) and the leading chicken tikka and tikkis (5.9 vs 6.0; 68%). Differences were not statistically significant. The Bayesian estimate put the chance they are rated about the same at 63% for patties and 52% for tikka and tikkis.
- **Leaders vs benchmark:** on average the 8 category leaders trailed their benchmark by only 0.3 points (patties −0.1, tikka −0.1, nuggets −0.2, seekh kebabs −0.3, keema −0.4, eggs −0.4, sausages −0.4, chicken chunks −0.6).
- **Category averages:** all lag the benchmark. Eggs (−1.2), chicken chunks (−0.8) and mutton keema (−0.8) lag most. Only 16–23% rated these "like very much", against 43–52% for the animal benchmark. Nuggets, patties, tikka and seekh kebabs were about 0.3 behind.
- **Product spread matters:** the best product (a chicken patty, 6.0) was rated "like very much" 3.7 times more often than the worst (an egg product, 4.3).
- **Eggs:** the three plant-based eggs scored 5.8, 5.0 and 4.3 against 6.2 for the real egg. 64% rated the best egg the same or better than the real one. This is NECTAR's first sensory data on eggs.

## R&D opportunities
- Flavor is the main gap. It was named as a dislike for 34% of plant-based products (27% for the plant-based leaders, 20% for animal benchmarks). Texture (22%) and appearance (5%) mattered less.
- "Animal-like flavor" (chicken-like, mutton-like, egg-like) was a top-5 opportunity in every one of the 8 categories. Richness, juiciness and "buttery" also ranked. Spiciness (masaledar) came up in four categories.
- Richness, earthy/grassy and buttery notes went along with animal-like flavors. Artificial, soya-like and unusual-aftertaste notes went against them.

## Concept and positioning
- 85% would buy the plant-based concept (5% would not), against 89% for the animal concept. Mean purchase intent 5.7 vs 5.8. Price and availability were not tested. Chicken sausages and patties scored at or above their animal counterparts. Chicken chunks had the biggest concept gap (−0.3).
- Plant-based products scored higher on animal welfare, environment and naturalness. Animal products scored higher on taste and protein.
- Protein: over 99% value protein and 74% want to eat more meat to get it. Protein was the most noticed item on the nutrition label (over twice the average) and lifted purchase intent by 0.42 points.

## Foreword context
- The share of Indians eating meat weekly rose from about a third to over half between 2006 and 2021 (NFHS-5).
- Argues India could become a hub for plant-based ingredients, since it is the world's largest producer, consumer and importer of pulses.

## Caveats
- Young, urban, mostly higher-income, in two cities only. Not a picture of India as a whole.
- Purchase intent is for the concept only. It says nothing about real purchases.
- Authors are advocates for the sector, not neutral parties.
