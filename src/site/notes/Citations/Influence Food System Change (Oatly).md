## Why you're reading this

We created Oatly with a purpose: to help people eat better and live healthier lives without recklessly taxing the planet's resources. We’ve remained committed to this mission for over 30 years, and for nearly a decade we've transparently reported on our environmental footprint. However, viewing our impact in isolation only goes so far.

So, this year, we’re taking a unique approach that goes beyond our traditional sustainability reporting. We interviewed 29 experts across the food system – researchers, farmers, processors, retailers, café owners and policymakers – to understand what changes they are already seeing and what the evidence suggests is most capable of altering consumer behaviours. Alongside this, we ran a survey with more than 12,000 consumers across the UK, USA, Germany, France, Sweden and the Netherlands to understand what people know, feel, and want to change about the future of the food system. Together, this has helped us at Oatly think more broadly about where to focus our efforts next, and ensure we're not just contributing, but driving meaningful change.

Within this document, we draw on expert insight, consumer polling and existing industry research to set out five themes aimed at the powerful middle – those that sit between farm and fork and at the policymakers and legislators who set the terms within which the middle actors operate – that, we believe, have the responsibility and power to drive real change across the food system through strategic changes to the consumer environment:

1. **Optimism is a strategy for success** – The foundation of progress is a belief that change is possible.
2. **Focus on taste** – Facts and logic don't make mouths water. Food choices are still largely driven by emotion, and investment in taste is key to driving desirability.
3. **Tackle misinformation** – Change is infinitely harder when people don't know what's true.
4. **Make the better choice the default** – Create new norms by opting out – not in – to the more sustainable alternative – and the public agrees.
5. **Address cost head-on** – A more equitable food environment for plant-rich diets – from subsidies to taxation – is required to level the playing field and shift norms.

## Consumer polling figures (verbatim from the report)

- "Around 80% say the future of the food system matters to them, but a third believe they worry about it more than others."
- "Our polling data shows over a quarter (26%) of people believe improving taste and quality is one of the most important things that need to change to make it easier for people to eat more healthily and sustainably."
- "[O]ur data shows the public holds food manufacturers most responsible for ensuring people eat healthily and sustainably (44%) - ahead of farmers (39%), supermarkets (37%), individuals themselves (35%) and government (32%)."
- "72% of the public think decisions about the health and sustainability of our food system are only “sometimes” or “rarely” based on science."
- "When asked to select up to three of the most important things that need to change, 38% chose ‘making sustainable and healthy options the default.’"
- "68% of respondents said they wouldn’t mind if oat drinks were the default in cafés, with most of that group asking only that cost and choice be preserved."
- "In our consumer polling, more than half (53%) named affordability and access as the biggest barriers."
- "[T]wo-thirds of respondents are open to it [oat drink] being the default in cafés, but 28% of that group said only if it doesn't cost them more."
