Vegan diets are consistently linked to weight loss and improvements in cardiometabolic health, yet the mechanisms driving these benefits are not fully understood. One factor that may help explain these associations is dietary energy density—the amount of energy per unit weight of food consumed. Diets lower in energy density have been shown to reduce overall energy intake and support weight loss, even without intentional calorie restriction. As reported previously, an ad libitum low-fat vegan diet resulted in weight loss and improved body composition in adults with overweight. Notably, these changes occurred without prescribed energy restriction, suggesting that underlying dietary properties may have facilitated a spontaneous reduction in energy intake. In this secondary analysis, we examined dietary energy density as a potential contributor. Because plant-based foods are typically lower in energy density due to their composition, we hypothesized that adoption of a vegan diet would reduce dietary energy density and, in turn, help explain the observed metabolic improvements.
