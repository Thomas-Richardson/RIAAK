# Executive Summary / Abstract
[From Executive Summary]

- This project evaluates the impact of increasing plant-based milk alternative availability on the demand for fluid dairy milk in the United States.
- The investigation consists of three primary analyses: data validation, research synthesis on price substitution, and dairy demand elasticity estimation.
- Researchers compared different sources of plant-based sales data to determine if plant-based growth is sufficient to replace declining dairy sales.
- The study summarized existing research to determine whether plant-based and dairy milks act as price substitutes for one another.
- Demand for whole and 2% dairy milk was estimated across separate periods between 2001–2019 to identify major changes in consumer responsiveness to price.
- Results confirm that while the volume of plant-based milk consumed has increased over time, it does not fully explain the observed decline in dairy milk consumption.
- Findings indicate that dairy sales are relatively insensitive to price fluctuations of plant-based milks.
- Plant-based milk sales are more responsive to price changes in lower-fat dairy milks than in higher-fat dairy milks.
- Tentative findings suggest that whole and 2% dairy milk consumption is decreasing even as prices for those products decrease.
- The study suggests that consumer responsiveness to dairy prices may have undergone structural changes in recent years.
- Unusual data patterns indicate that the dairy demand model requires further refinement before drawing definitive conclusions.
- Overall results suggest that the decline in dairy milk demand cannot be confidently attributed solely to the expansion of plant-based milk products.
