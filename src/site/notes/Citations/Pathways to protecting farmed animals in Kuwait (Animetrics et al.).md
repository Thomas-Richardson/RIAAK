# Executive Summary / Abstract
[From Executive Summary]

- Attitudes toward plant-based eating are generally positive, with many respondents viewing plant-based diets as healthy and expressing interest in substituting plant-based proteins for animal protein.
- Advocacy organizations can leverage this openness to encourage corporations, institutions, and food-service providers to improve the availability and quality of plant-based options.
- There is widespread and strong public support for laws and regulations that protect farmed animals and regulate their treatment.
- Advocates may find it useful to emphasize to policymakers and regulators that legal protections—such as minimum space requirements and veterinary care—are supported by a clear majority.
- Animal protection organizations receive broad public support, though most supporters currently prefer low-effort engagement like following or sharing content on social media.
- Advocacy groups should align outreach strategies with how supporters prefer to engage, providing multiple levels of participation from simple online actions to volunteering or donating.
- Key barriers to progress include a strong attachment to meat, the belief that eating meat is an unquestionable right, and speciesist views that prioritize human interests over animals.
- Addressing these difficult-to-shift beliefs requires evidence-informed strategies that are carefully tested and sensitive to the local social and cultural context.
- Individuals who recognize animals as sentient and are aware of the broader negative impacts of industrial agriculture tend to show greater openness to dietary change and policy reform.
- Advocacy efforts may benefit from emphasizing farmed animals’ capacity to feel emotions while communicating how industrial agriculture affects human health and the environment.
- Government institutions, certification bodies, and religious institutions are commonly perceived as having a responsibility to educate the public about farmed animal welfare.
- Advocacy efforts can be strengthened by communicating these public expectations to institutional and religious actors to encourage discussion of farmed animal protections.
