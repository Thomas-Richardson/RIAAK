# Executive Summary / Abstract
[From Executive Summary]

- Industrial animal agriculture in Lebanon is associated with significant harms, including large-scale animal suffering, environmental degradation, public health risks, and worker exploitation.
- This study addresses a critical lack of locally grounded data in the West Asia and North Africa (WANA/MENA) region regarding public attitudes toward animal agriculture.
- Although most respondents currently consume predominantly animal-based diets, attitudes toward plant-based eating are generally positive and often viewed as healthy.
- There is widespread and strong public support for laws and regulations that protect farmed animals and regulate their treatment, such as minimum space requirements and veterinary care.
- Animal protection organizations receive broad public support, though most supporters currently prefer low-effort forms of engagement like social media interaction.
- Strong attachment to meat and the belief that eating meat is an unquestionable right emerge as key barriers to progress for farmed animal protections.
- Speciesist views that prioritize human interests over those of animals are also identified as a significant barrier to systemic change.
- Recognition of animals as sentient beings is a strong facilitator for change, as these individuals show greater openness to dietary shifts and policy reform.
- Awareness of the broader negative impacts of industrial animal agriculture—such as climate change and pandemic risks—is associated with stronger support for advocacy and technological innovation.
- Government institutions, certification bodies, and religious institutions are commonly perceived by the public as having a responsibility to educate people about farmed animal welfare.
- Advocacy organizations are encouraged to leverage favorable views of plant-based eating to drive availability and uptake in corporations and food-service providers.
- Advocates should emphasize that legal protections for animals are supported by a clear majority of the public when engaging with policymakers and regulators.
- Outreach strategies should provide multiple levels of engagement, ranging from simple online actions to more active participation like volunteering or donating.
- Messaging for animal protection should center on animals' capacity to feel emotions while communicating how industrial agriculture affects human health and the environment.
- Advocacy efforts can be strengthened by communicating public expectations for animal welfare education to institutional and religious actors.
