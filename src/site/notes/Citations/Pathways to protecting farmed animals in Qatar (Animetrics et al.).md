# Executive Summary / Abstract
[From Executive Summary]

- Industrial animal agriculture is associated with significant harms including large-scale animal suffering, environmental degradation, public health risks, and worker exploitation.
- This study addresses the lack of locally grounded data in the West Asia and North Africa (WANA/MENA) region regarding public attitudes toward animal agriculture and pathways to change.
- While most respondents currently consume animal-based diets, 71% hold generally positive attitudes toward plant-based eating.
- Many respondents view plant-based diets as healthy and express interest in learning more about plant-based proteins or substituting them for animal protein.
- There is widespread and strong public support (nearly 87%) for laws and regulations that protect farmed animals and regulate their treatment.
- Legal protections supported by the public include minimum space requirements, access to food and water, and regular veterinary care.
- Animal protection organizations receive broad support from 57% of the public, though most supporters prefer low-effort engagement like following social media.
- Supporters of animal advocacy are often open to higher-level engagement, including signing petitions, volunteering, or making donations.
- Key barriers to progress include a strong attachment to meat, the belief that eating meat is an unquestionable right, and speciesist views that prioritize humans over animals.
- Respondents who recognize animals as sentient and understand the negative impacts of industrial agriculture show greater openness to dietary change and legal protections.
- Messaging centered on animal sentience and the systemic impacts of animal agriculture on health and the environment is recommended for advocacy efforts.
- Government institutions, certification bodies, and religious institutions are commonly perceived as having a responsibility to educate the public about animal welfare.
- Advocacy organizations are encouraged to leverage consumer insights to encourage corporations and food-service providers to offer more plant-based options.
- Outreach strategies should be aligned with how supporters prefer to engage, providing multiple levels of participation from simple online actions to volunteering.
