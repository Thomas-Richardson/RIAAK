# Executive Summary / Abstract
[From Executive Summary]

- Current dietary patterns are largely animal-based, but approximately 70% of respondents hold positive attitudes toward plant-based diets.
- Nearly 80% of the public agrees that farmed animals should have legal protections governing their treatment, such as minimum space and veterinary care.
- There is significant support for advocacy, with 56% of respondents stating they would support organizations protecting farmed animals and promoting plant-based eating.
- A substantial minority (32%) expresses support for cultivated meat, indicating openness to technological food alternatives.
- Positive attitudes toward meat are a primary barrier, with 96% of respondents viewing meat consumption as natural, normal, necessary, or part of family tradition.
- Entitlement beliefs are widespread, as 87% of respondents consider eating meat to be an "unquestionable right."
- Speciesist views are present, with 51% believing that concern for farmed animals is excessive compared to human problems.
- Recognition of animal sentience is a key facilitator for change, though respondents are more likely to acknowledge positive emotions (65%) than the capacity for pain or suffering (36-43%).
- Awareness of the broader negative impacts of industrial animal agriculture, such as climate change and pandemic risks, is moderate at 46%.
- Government institutions (85%) and certification bodies (84%) are perceived as the entities most responsible for educating the public on animal welfare.
- Supporters of animal protection favor low-commitment digital engagement, such as following (71%) or sharing content (74%) on social media.
- Many supporters are open to more active participation, including signing petitions (64%), volunteering (62%), or making donations (59%).
- Advocates are encouraged to leverage high public support for legal protections when engaging with policymakers and regulators.
- Messaging should prioritize animal sentience and the systemic impacts of animal agriculture on human health, communities, and the environment.
- Advocacy organizations can benefit from using consumer interest in plant-based proteins to encourage food-service providers to increase the availability and quality of options.
