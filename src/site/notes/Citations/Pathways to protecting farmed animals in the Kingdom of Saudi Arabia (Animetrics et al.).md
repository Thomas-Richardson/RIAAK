# Executive Summary / Abstract
[From Executive Summary]

- Current diets in Saudi Arabia remain largely animal-based, yet approximately 70% of respondents hold positive attitudes toward plant-based eating.
- There is strong public support for the policy pathway, with nearly 80% of respondents agreeing that farmed animals should have legal protections.
- More than half of the public (56%) expresses support for advocacy organizations that work to protect farmed animals and promote plant-based eating.
- Interest in dietary shifts is notable, with 55% of respondents interested in learning more about plant-based proteins and 44% willing to substitute them for animal protein.
- Support for technological innovation exists as a substantial minority, with 32% of respondents supporting the development of cultivated meat.
- Positive attitudes toward meat are a primary barrier, with 96% of respondents viewing meat consumption as natural, normal, necessary, or delicious.
- Entitlement beliefs are widespread, as 87% of respondents believe that eating meat is an "unquestionable right" for every person.
- Recognition of animal sentience is uneven; while 65% believe farmed animals can feel positive emotions, only 36% specifically agree that they can feel pain.
- Awareness of the broader impacts of industrial animal agriculture—such as climate change and public health risks—is linked to greater support for dietary and policy changes.
- Government institutions (85%) and certification bodies (84%) are viewed as the entities most responsible for educating the public on animal welfare.
- Supporters of animal protection prefer digital and low-commitment engagement, such as following social media (71%) and sharing content (74%).
- Advocacy organizations should leverage the high public demand for legal protections to engage with policymakers and regulatory bodies.
- Messaging should center on animal sentience and communicate how industrial animal agriculture impacts human health, communities, and the environment.
- Organizations can use positive consumer interest to encourage food-service providers and corporations to increase the availability of high-quality plant-based options.
