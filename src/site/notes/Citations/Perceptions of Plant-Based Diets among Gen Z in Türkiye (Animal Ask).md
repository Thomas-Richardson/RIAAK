# Executive Summary / Abstract
[From Summary section]

- 18–24-year-olds in Türkiye view plant-based diets more negatively than middle-aged groups, specifically regarding health and nutrition, and practicality and access.
- Negative perceptions among this group center mostly on health and cost, while views on values and social norms are relatively positive.
- Among young people who report reducing or avoiding animal-based products, the most common motivations are respect for animal rights and health.
- Young men hold more negative views than women regarding the health, values, and taste dimensions of plant-based diets.
- Negative perceptions about practicality and access are significantly more common among students than among employed young people.
- Although a barrier for many, health is a top motivation for those already reducing animal product consumption, suggesting it is a priority topic for advocacy.
- Campaigns providing accurate, reliable information on the nutritional value of plant-based diets are recommended to help reduce negative health perceptions.
- The gender gap is largest regarding values and social norms, where nearly half of young men hold negative views compared to a quarter of women.
- Advocacy aimed at students should focus on increasing the availability of affordable and easily accessible plant-based options.
- Exploring values-based messages concerning the environment or ethics may help develop more effective strategies for engaging young men.
- Experience-based approaches, such as food tastings, are suggested to address negative perceptions of taste among young men.
- Familiarity with plant-based diets tends to occur alongside more positive perceptions, though the report notes this is a correlation rather than a proven cause.
- Understanding these perceptions is vital for advocacy as young people's developing habits will shape future consumption patterns in Türkiye.
- The report acknowledges limitations, including a non-representative sample and small sub-sample sizes for specific questions on motivations.
