# Executive Summary / Abstract
[From Executive Summary]

- Reducing meat consumption is critical for the sake of the planet, public health and animal suffering.
- Social media advertising provides an excellent opportunity to facilitate this by encouraging consumers to make dietary changes.
- This study ran advertisements on social media in four UK areas to sway shoppers away from pork.
- Supermarket retail data was collected to assess how consumption changed during and after advertising compared to the rest of the UK and politically matched areas.
- Fresh pork sales dropped significantly after the advertising campaign in target areas compared to the rest of the UK, falling by an estimated 1.3% (p < .001).
- Sales of all fresh meat (including pork) were approximately 2.3% higher (p < .001) where advertising occurred compared to the UK as a whole.
- The increase in overall meat sales was reversed when advertisement regions were matched against politically similar control areas.
- Results showed decreases in both pork and overall meat consumption for both left-wing and right-wing areas exposed to the advertisements when compared to matched controls.
- Secondary analyses indicated that a “Make the switch to plant-based” tagline was more impactful than a “Leave pork off your plate” tagline in the advertisements.
- These findings provide behavioural evidence that social media advertising can successfully influence supermarket meat sales.
