# Executive Summary / Abstract
[From Executive Summary]

- This paper provides an overview of the legislative landscape for pig protection in the EU and recommends the prohibition of farrowing crates.
- The proposed policy scenario assumes a ban announced in 2028 with a 10-year transition period concluding in 2038.
- Modeling focuses on 22 Member States, as Sweden, Germany, Austria, Denmark, and Finland already have national legislation phasing out farrowing crates.
- The central cost estimate for transitioning to free-farrowing is approximately EUR 0.008 (less than 1 cent) per kg of pig meat.
- The aggregate net policy cost for the transition is estimated at EUR 1.11 billion through the modeling horizon of 2060.
- Increased costs are primarily attributed to higher building expenses per sow and greater labor requirements.
- Higher transition costs are partially offset by faster piglet weight gain observed in free-farrowing systems.
- An EU-wide ban would avoid an estimated 7.63 billion sow-crate-days during the assessment period.
- The aggregate cost-effectiveness of the policy is calculated at EUR 0.15 per sow-crate-day avoided.
- By 2060, the counterfactual scenario still projects roughly 206 million ongoing sow-crate-days per year without a ban.
- Animal welfare impacts are measured using Animal Quality-Adjusted Life Years (AQALY) across nutrition, environment, health, and behavior domains.
- Moving a single sow from a crate to a free-farrowing system yields an annualized improvement of 1.07 AQALY.
- Using a neuron-proportional sentience weight based on pig-to-human cortical neuron ratios (1/44), the monetized welfare benefit is valued at EUR 44 billion.
- The resulting benefit-cost ratio is 40, indicating that every EUR 1 of cost incurred delivers EUR 40 in sow welfare benefits.
- Alternative valuation approaches based on consumer willingness-to-pay and hedonic capacity produce even higher benefit-cost ratios of 376 and 908, respectively.
- Across all three monetization frameworks evaluated, the net social balance of the proposed ban remains positive by one to three orders of magnitude.
