A 12-week randomized clinical trial found that adults with type 1 diabetes (T1D) on a low-fat, vegan diet (n=29) showed greater improvements in insulin sensitivity compared to a portion-controlled diet (n=29). This secondary analysis calculates the greenhouse gas emissions (GHGE) and cumulative energy demand (CED) of both diets by linking 3-day dietary records to external data sources. A repeated-measures ANOVA was performed unadjusted and adjusted for change in energy intake. GHGE decreased in the vegan group by 55% (p=0.001), compared with no change in the portion-controlled group (between-group p=0.01). CED decreased in the vegan group by 44% (p=0.01), with no change in the portion-controlled group (between-group p=0.22). Energy intake was not a significant predictor of the observed changes. The decrease in GHGE and CED in the vegan group likely reflects reduced meat and dairy intake, suggesting that prioritizing plant-based foods over animal products may effectively reduce dietary environmental impact.
