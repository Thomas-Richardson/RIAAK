# Executive Summary / Abstract
[From Executive Summary]

- This report examines the challenges and opportunities for effective farmed animal advocacy in Ethiopia, with the goal of providing actionable interventions to improve animal welfare.
- It addresses the rise of industrial animal agriculture (IAA) in Ethiopia, a key player in African agriculture, aligning with efforts to build local advocacy capacity and reduce animal suffering.
- Based on desk research, expert interviews, and a prioritisation process, the report provides three top recommendations for advocates:
- **Chicken welfare regulations**: Drafting and advocating for the adoption of regulations could pre-empt the entrenchment of low-welfare practices as the country moves toward greater intensification.
- This approach could complement the recently enacted animal health and welfare bill, requiring advocates to account for common challenges associated with law and policy interventions.
- **Implementation of OIE standards**: This intervention would prioritise training producers on OIE guidelines to address welfare concerns for cattle and small ruminants across livestock value chains.
- **Cage-free directory**: A directory could help stem the shift from cage-free to caged systems, particularly among urban and peri-urban producers, by providing incentives to remain cage-free.
- The report also discusses two additional interventions characterized by a higher degree of uncertainty at this stage:
- **Radio campaigns**: Provide a low-cost, high-reach form of preventative public outreach suited to shaping norms and attitudes early, though likely impact remains uncertain.
- **Alternative proteins**: A preventative strategy aimed at slowing future growth in animal-product demand by improving the distribution and accessibility of products.
- These recommendations serve as a roadmap for advocates interested in pursuing farmed animal welfare work in Ethiopia, particularly for early intervention in sectors likely to shape long-term outcomes.
