# Executive Summary / Abstract
[From Key Takeaways]

- Healthier food remains nearly twice as expensive per calorie as less healthy food, and the gap is widening.
- Households with children in the lowest income quintile would need to spend 85% of their disposable income on food to afford the Eatwell Guide diet.
- Pre-prepared and convenient plant-based alternative food products are on average 56% more expensive per calorie than equivalent meat products.
- Fast-food outlets make up 1 in 4 places to buy food in England, rising to over 1 in 3 in the most deprived areas.
- Only 1 in 10 children’s breakfast cereals are high in fibre, while 1 in 5 are high in sugar.
- 40% of food and non-alcoholic drink promotions are on foods high in fat, salt, or sugar (HFSS).
- Fruit and vegetables account for only 3% of traditional advertising food and drink expenditure, while unhealthy foods account for at least 29%.
- 74% of baby and toddler snacks with a front-of-pack claim still have medium or high levels of sugar.
- In an average English Reception class of 30 children, three will be living with obesity; in Year 6, this more than doubles to seven children.
- 10-year-olds from the most deprived backgrounds are over 1.5 times more likely to have short stature.
- More than 1 in 5 five-year-olds in England have tooth decay, rising to 1 in 3 in the most deprived areas.
- Healthy life expectancy is at its lowest level since 2013–15, with a nearly 20-year gap between the least and most deprived areas.
- Fewer than 1 in 10 teenagers eat five portions of fruit and vegetables a day, while 95% consume above recommended levels of free sugar.
- People in the most deprived groups are more than twice as likely to have diets lacking in three or more key micronutrients.
- The food system is lagging on climate goals, with emissions down just 22% since 2008 compared to a 41% drop across the UK economy.
- An estimated 204km²—an area larger than Glasgow—was deforested in 2023 to produce agricultural products linked to the UK economy.
- Government should ensure benefit and wage levels cover the cost of a healthy diet and increase them in line with food inflation.
- Local authority planning powers should be strengthened to curb the proliferation of unhealthy fast-food outlets.
- Mandatory reporting of healthy sales and fruit and vegetable sales should be introduced for all large businesses.
- Advertising regulations should be expanded to include brand and outdoor advertising, and volume-price promotion restrictions should be expanded to include price promotions.
