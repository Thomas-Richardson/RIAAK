# Executive Summary / Abstract
[From Summary section]

- Most animal advocates are satisfied with most aspects of their work, but there is room for improvement in areas such as leadership, pay, and workload.
- Advocates stay with animal advocacy organizations for a median of 2.3 years before moving on or leaving.
- The most common reasons for leaving roles include problems with leadership (40%), finding better opportunities (35%), dissatisfaction with the type of advocacy performed (27%), and burnout (21%).
- Approximately 85% of advocates have left at least one advocacy role in the past, and 11% have left the animal protection movement entirely.
- For paid advocates, the strongest determinants of retention are reasonable job demands, a good fit with organizational values, and satisfaction with available resources and support.
- Satisfaction with organizational leadership and feeling that one is making a difference are also critical factors for retaining paid staff.
- One-third of paid advocates reported being dissatisfied with aspects of their organization’s leadership.
- Up to 25% of paid advocates experience compassion fatigue, including burnout and secondary traumatic stress.
- More than one-third of paid advocates are unhappy with their pay, benefits, and opportunities for career advancement or training.
- For unpaid advocates, retention is most strongly influenced by satisfaction with leadership, a sense of identification with other advocates, and a safe environment.
- While most unpaid advocates are satisfied with leadership, 13% have experienced harassment, bullying, or abuse from other advocates or the public.
- Significant gaps in support for unpaid advocates exist, with 33% reporting insufficient training and over 50% reporting a lack of support for career advancement.
- In the past five years, 49% of paid advocates and 28% of unpaid advocates have experienced discrimination or harassment in an advocacy context.
- Members of marginalized groups are disproportionately impacted by unfair treatment, including 50% of advocates with disabilities and 29% of advocates of color.
- One-third of female and non-binary advocates reported experiencing discrimination, unfair treatment, or harassment based on their group membership.
