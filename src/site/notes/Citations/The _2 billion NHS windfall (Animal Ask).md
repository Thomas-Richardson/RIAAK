# Executive Summary / Abstract
[From Executive Summary]

- In 2024, Britain faces key challenges in meeting environmental targets and improving the health of the British people.
- Spiralling health costs and a tough economic climate require the government and families to do more with less.
- Modest reductions in meat consumption can provide strong benefits to both the government and the average family.
- This report reviews literature in public health, climate science, economics, and behavioural science to model the benefits of several meat-reduction scenarios.
- Reductions in meat consumption can save the NHS money by reducing deaths from key lifestyle diseases such as cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, obesity, and cancer.
- If the British population ate meat-free lunches on weekdays, improved health could save the NHS as much as £2.2 billion annually.
- Reducing British meat consumption by 10% would offset the emissions of 16% of the cars on UK roads.
- The average British household could replace 20% of their meat consumption while saving over £130 annually.
- Implementing meat-free defaults in public catering would save the NHS £74 million a year.
- The report aims to educate both policymakers and the public on the personal and societal benefits of eating less meat so individuals can make informed dietary choices.
