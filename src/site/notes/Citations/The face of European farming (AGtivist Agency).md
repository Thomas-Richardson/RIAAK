A Europe-wide investigation by a consortium of journalists has uncovered that intensive livestock production is sweeping across the continent, raising concerns about animal welfare, pollution, disease spread, and the economic impact on conventional farmers.

Key findings include:
- For the first time, more than 24,000 large-scale pig and poultry units have been identified and mapped across Europe.
- Thousands of intensive farms were approved over the past decade, with hotspots in Spain, Italy, France, Germany, Poland, the Netherlands, and the UK.
- Some poultry units house over 1.4 million chickens, and the largest pig farms confine more than 30,000 animals.
- The investigation has prompted accusations that the EU’s agricultural subsidy program (CAP) favors larger, industrial farms.
- Critics highlight a \\"cascade of harms,\\" including environmental degradation, negative health impacts like antimicrobial resistance, and the decline of small and medium-sized farms.
- Supporters of intensive farming argue it is necessary for food security and that larger farms often have high standards and investments to mitigate welfare problems.
