# Executive Summary / Abstract
[From Key Takeaways]

- Most respondents (83.5%) report following an unrestricted omnivorous diet, while 14.9% report reducing or avoiding animal-based products.
- Fully plant-based diets remain relatively niche, with 0.5% of the sample identifying as vegan, 3.1% as vegetarian, and 0.3% as pescatarian.
- Negative perceptions of plant-based diets are most prevalent regarding health, nutrition, affordability, and practicality.
- More than half of respondents (60.3%) do not consider a plant-based diet to be healthy, and 52.2% do not believe it is affordable.
- Health is the most commonly cited motivation for reducing animal-based consumption (63.2%), followed by animal welfare (54.3%).
- Health concerns play a dual role, acting as both a primary source of hesitation for some and a leading driver of change for others.
- Unrestricted omnivores report substantially more negative views toward plant-based diets—particularly regarding health and taste—compared to those already reducing animal products.
- Younger consumers (aged 18–24) report relatively high levels of negative perceptions concerning the health and practicality of plant-based diets.
- A clear geographic pattern exists in Türkiye, with negative perceptions of plant-based diets being more concentrated in eastern regions.
- Increased familiarity and regular exposure to plant-based alternatives are strongly associated with more positive views and dietary change.
- Men are more likely than women to hold negative perceptions of plant-based diets and are less likely to reduce their animal product consumption.
- Perceptions of practicality may depend on whether a plant-based diet is framed as using specialized alternatives or familiar everyday foods like legumes.
- Communication efforts should prioritize providing clear, accessible information on health to correct common misconceptions.
- Framing plant-based eating around affordable, accessible, and familiar options may help overcome perceived barriers to adoption.
- Animal welfare should be leveraged as a key point of engagement, as it is a significant motivator for consumers already making dietary shifts.
- Outreach and messaging should be tailored by gender, age, and region to account for meaningful differences in attitudes across groups.
