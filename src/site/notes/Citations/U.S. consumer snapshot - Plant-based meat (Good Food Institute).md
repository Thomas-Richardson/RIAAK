# Executive Summary / Abstract
[AI-derived summary]

*   **Awareness and Trial:** Plant-based meat is familiar to most U.S. consumers, with approximately two-thirds having heard of it and three-fifths having tried it at least once. However, it is not top-of-mind, with fewer than half hearing about it regularly.
*   **Consumption:** Around 40% of Americans have eaten plant-based meat in the past year, with 24% eating it monthly or more frequently. Consumption is highest among male, younger (Gen Z, Millennial), non-white, and urban consumers.
*   **Target Audience:** The key target market is omnivores who still frequently eat conventional meat. Most consumers of plant-based meat do not identify as vegetarian or vegan, and a majority do not identify as flexitarians. Among those who ate plant-based meat in the past year, 70% also report eating meat.
*   **Consumer Perceptions:** A majority of Americans perceive plant-based meat as healthy (especially low in cholesterol and saturated fat), high in protein, and sustainable. However, compared to conventional meat, it is widely seen as less tasty, less natural, and having less understandable ingredients.
*   **Barriers to Adoption:** The primary reasons lapsed consumers stopped eating plant-based meat are dislike of the taste/texture (40%) and high cost (37%). For those who have never tried it, the top reasons are assuming they won't like the taste/texture (60%) and not seeing a reason to eat it (52%).
*   **Motivators for Purchase:** The most convincing factors for trying a new plant-based meat product would be the taste and texture being exactly like conventional meat (31%) and a lower cost than conventional meat (27%).
*   **Growth Opportunities:** Re-attracting lapsed buyers and increasing purchase frequency among current users are key to category growth. Few consumers (9%) reported trying plant-based meat for the first time in 2025, emphasizing the need for retention and re-engagement of those already familiar with the category.
