# Executive Summary / Abstract
[From Executive Summary]

- Ultra-processed foods (UPFs) now account for more than 50% of the average diet in some countries, including the United Kingdom and the United States.
- The range of UPFs is continually expanding and includes common items such as bread, cereals, condiments, sweets, cakes, soft drinks, and packaged meals.
- Despite widely acknowledged health risks, products from intensive animal agriculture are typically only considered UPFs if excessive processing takes place after an animal has been slaughtered.
- Approximately 80 billion animals—ten times the total human population—are slaughtered for food every year.
- Most of these animals undergo significant "ultra-processing" during their lives before they are killed.
- The report introduces the term "Ultra-Processed Animals" (UPAs) to identify and address the systemic issue of pre-slaughter processing.
- The report argues that it is time to acknowledge and address the far-reaching consequences of the UPA phenomenon.
- The primary policy recommendation is the implementation of mandatory labelling for all foods derived from UPAs.
- This proposed labelling is modeled after existing public health warnings used for alcohol and tobacco products.
- Mandatory labelling is presented as a bare-minimum and eminently achievable policy response to the risks posed by UPAs.
