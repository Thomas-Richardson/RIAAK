The average German two-person household could cut its annual food spending by around €1,315 by switching to a diet built around the [Planetary Health Diet (PHD)](https://vegconomist.com/topic/planetary-health-diet), according to a study from food-systems campaign organization [Madre Brava](https://vegconomist.com/organisations-and-brands/madre-brava/) and analytics firm PATOS Solutions.

The report, [Healthy Returns: What the Planetary Health Diet Means for German Wallets and Wellbeing](https://www.madrebrava.org/latest/better-for-wallets-wellbeing-and-healthcare-new-study-reveals-the-benefits-of-plant-forward-diets-in-germany), compares a typical German shopping basket with one aligned to the PHD, the science-based dietary model developed by the EAT-Lancet Commission to balance human health with environmental limits. A PHD-aligned basket for a two-person household costs about €73.14 per week, roughly €25 below the current average shop of €98.36.

The gap is widest in the protein category. Daily spending on protein sources drops from €2.47 in a typical basket to €1.55 in the PHD version, a difference the study attributes to a shift toward legumes, nuts and seeds.

### Health outcomes tied to the diet shift

Beyond household budgets, the study models health effects. A PHD-aligned diet could add almost two healthy years of life per person over an 80-year lifespan and prevent 200,000 premature deaths annually in [Germany](https://vegconomist.com/region/europe/germany/), according to the authors. They put the associated annual savings in direct and indirect healthcare costs at roughly €53 billion, which the study notes exceeds the funding gap that Germany’s statutory health insurance system is projected to reach by 2030 without reform.

Two independent modeling approaches produced consistent results, according to PATOS Solutions Managing Director Dr Amelie Michalke. “Two independent scientific models arrive at the same result on an issue that affects every household in Germany. That’s the kind of solid evidence that makes credible recommendations for policy and business possible in the first place. Health outcomes can no longer be a footnote in how we calculate the true cost of our food,” she said.

### Retailers hold the largest lever

Aldi Süd, Edeka, Lidl and Rewe, which together account for around 80% of the German grocery market, contributed to the study by outlining measures already underway. Edeka is adding plant-based products to its Edeka Herzstücke range and publishing PHD-aligned recipes and meal plans, while Aldi Süd has assembled its own nutrition team and more than doubled its range of fully plant-based labeled products since 2021. Most of the retailers now calculate their protein split, the ratio of animal to plant protein sales, and Rewe and Lidl have set targets to raise their plant-based share.

The study argues that Germany still lacks a single industry-wide dietary target, despite recommendations from the German Nutrition Society (DGE) and the EAT-Lancet Commission. It calls on supermarkets to commit to a protein sales ratio of 38% plant-based to 62% animal-based by 2035, and a 32% plant-based share for dairy and dairy alternatives. Contributors including ProVeg, the Albert Schweitzer Foundation, the Netzwerk Ernährungsräte, the Verbraucher Initiative and Paritätischer Wohlfahrtsverband point to a lack of coordination behind the shift toward healthier diets.

> “Supermarkets can be a source of stability, if retailers step up to that responsibility”

Florian Wall, Madre Brava’s Associate Director Germany, stated, “The fact that a colorful, healthy, delicious diet can already be cheaper today proves that, in a time of rising prices and overlapping crises, supermarkets can be a source of stability, if retailers step up to that responsibility.”

“That means the whole industry agreeing on one shared target for a more plant-based range, so that healthy eating stops being a matter of chance and becomes the default. Germany’s consumers deserve the same information, the same message, and the same access to healthy protein, no matter which supermarket they shop in.”
