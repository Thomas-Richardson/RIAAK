This is another of our guest-hosted episodes, where I hand the mic to someone who knows a particular area much better than I do. Today’s host is Elissa Lane, CEO at Global Food Partners, who works with farmers and food companies across Asia to help them transition to cage-free.

Elissa speaks with Janet Helms, Senior Manager of Global Sustainable Food Sourcing, Animal Health and Welfare at McDonald’s, where she leads the company’s global animal welfare strategy, including its cage-free and broiler welfare commitment work. Janet is a vet by training. She started out caring for dairy herds of up to 3,700 cows, then moved into animal welfare certification and sustainable sourcing at IKEA before joining McDonald’s. Importantly, these are Janet’s personal views and don't represent McDonald’s!

As you might imagine, working for McDonald’s, Janet was relatively guarded about what she shared. Nonetheless, I think this is a pretty interesting insight into how large food companies are improving animal welfare and what the people who work there are thinking. We don’t often get to hear from someone inside one of the world’s largest food companies, so I think this will be useful for anyone working on corporate advocacy.

Elissa and Janet talk about how McDonald’s US went fully cage-free ahead of its 2025 deadline, what suppliers need before they’ll invest millions in new barns, and why McDonald’s doesn’t have a global cage-free policy. Janet also covers some newer work on broiler welfare, including lighting that gives chickens darker areas to take cover in, and AI tools that help farmers monitor their birds.

Finally, they discuss what Janet wishes animal activists understood about how change happens inside big companies. Janet even turns the tables a few times and asks Alyssa what she’s learned from helping farmers in Asia go cage-free.

Enjoy the conversation!
