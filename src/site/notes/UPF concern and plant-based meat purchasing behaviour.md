# UPF concern and plant-based meat purchasing behaviour

GFI work looking at actual purchasing behaviour (not stated attitudes) found a useful disconnect:

- Consumers who are concerned about ultra-processed foods do buy fewer UPFs than people who are less concerned. So the concern translates into real behaviour for UPFs generally.
- But they do NOT buy less plant-based meat. If anything the opposite: health-conscious consumers tend to buy MORE plant-based meat alternatives than non-health-conscious consumers.

## Why this matters

If the UPF narrative isn't actually suppressing plant-based meat sales at the till, then the category slowdown can't be pinned on the "ultra-processed" framing. That would point to the slowdown being mostly the industry deflating after the hype cycle, rather than a health-narrative backlash hurting demand.

This is the empirical purchasing-behaviour angle on the broader story in [[The Ultra-Processed Myth\|The Ultra-Processed Myth]] and [[ultraprocessed is misleading\|ultraprocessed is misleading]]. It also connects to [[Are meat alternatives healthier than meat\|Are meat alternatives healthier than meat]] and [[What We Know About UK Plant-Based Meat Consumers\|What We Know About UK Plant-Based Meat Consumers]].
